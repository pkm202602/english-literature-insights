The Old English Period (450–1066)

The Old English Period, also known as the dawn of English literature, marks the historical era following the migration of Germanic tribes to Britain. This period laid the foundational stones for the English language and its literary traditions.

Key Characteristics:

  • Oral Tradition: Most literature was spoken or sung by scops (poets) before being written down.
  • Heroic Poetry: Focused on praise of warriors, bravery, loyalty to the king, and fate (Wyrd).
  • Christian Influences: A blend of pagan beliefs and Christian themes as the region converted to Christianity.

Major Literary Works

The most celebrated epic of this era is Beowulf, a monumental heroic poem that depicts the triumphs and struggles of the hero Beowulf against monsters. Other notable works include The Wanderer, The Seafarer, and the religious poems of Caedmon and Cynewulf.
