1832 – 1901 A.D.

The Victorian Period

An age of industrial expansion, social reform novels, moral earnestness, scientific crisis, and aesthetic rebellion under Queen Victoria.

Core Literary Movements & Innovations

Rise of the Social Reform Novel

Prose fiction became the dominant genre, addressing industrialization, class divides, child labor, and women's social roles.

Moral Earnestness & Doubt

Literary tension between religious faith, utilitarian morality, and scientific discoveries (Darwinian evolution).

Serialized Publication

Novels published in affordable monthly installments in periodicals, creating cliffhangers and widespread public readership.

Pre-Raphaelitism & Aestheticism

Rebellion against industrial commercialism via medieval art revival, sensory richness, and later "art for art's sake."

Sub-Periods & Literary Movements

1832 – 1848 A.D.

Early Victorian Period

Defined by social unrest, industrial growth, political reform acts, and the birth of social-problem fiction.

Key Figures: Early Charles Dickens, Elizabeth Gaskell, and the Brontë sisters.
1848 – 1870 A.D.

High Victorian Period

Peak economic prosperity, imperial expansion, religious debate, dramatic monologues, and philosophical essay writing.

Key Figures: Alfred Lord Tennyson, Robert Browning, George Eliot, and Matthew Arnold.
1848 – 1860 A.D.

Pre-Raphaelite Movement

An artistic and poetic brotherhood seeking detailed realism, medieval symbolism, and vivid sensual imagery in poetry.

Key Figures: Dante Gabriel Rossetti, Christina Rossetti, and William Morris.
1870 – 1901 A.D.

Late Victorian / Aestheticism

Characterized by Victorian decline, pessimistic realism, gothic revival, wit, decadence, and the "Art for Art's Sake" movement.

Key Figures: Oscar Wilde, Thomas Hardy, Robert Louis Stevenson, and Walter Pater.
