Reference Guide

About English Literature

A structural breakdown of the major divisions, historical periods, and sub-ages of the English literary canon.

1. Old (Ancient) English Period

450 – 1066 A.D.

Anglo-Saxon invasion, heroic oral poetry, Christian monastic scribes, and manuscripts such as Beowulf.

2. Middle English Period

1066 – 1500 A.D.
The Anglo-Norman Period 1066 – 1340 A.D.
The Age of Chaucer 1340 – 1400 A.D.
Dark / Barren Period 1400 – 1485/1500 A.D.

3. The Renaissance / Early Modern Period

1500 – 1660 A.D.
Preparation of Renaissance 1500 – 1558 A.D.
The Elizabethan Period 1558 – 1603 A.D.
The Jacobean Period 1603 – 1625 A.D. (Includes Age of Shakespeare: 1590–1616 A.D.)
The Caroline Period 1625 – 1649 A.D.
The Commonwealth Period 1649 – 1660 A.D.
Puritan Period (Overlapping) 1620 – 1660 A.D.

4. Neo-Classical Period

1660 – 1798 A.D.
The Restoration Period 1660 – 1700 A.D. (Age of John Dryden)
The Augustan Period 1700 – 1745 A.D. (Age of Pope & Swift)
The Age of Sensibility 1745 – 1785/1798 A.D. (Age of Reason / Dr. Johnson)

5. Romantic Period

1798 – 1832 A.D.

Emphasis on nature, emotion, individualism, imagination, and reaction against Enlightenment rationalism.

6. Victorian Period

1832 – 1901 A.D.
The Pre-Raphaelites 1848 – 1860 A.D.
Aestheticism and Decadence 1880 – 1901 A.D.

7. Modern Period (1st Half of 20th Century)

1901 – 1939 A.D.
The Edwardian Period 1901 – 1910 A.D.
The Georgian Period 1910 – 1936 A.D.

8. Post-Modern Period (2nd Half of 20th Century)

1939 – Present

Meta-fiction, post-colonialism, Theatre of the Absurd, and contemporary literary trends.
