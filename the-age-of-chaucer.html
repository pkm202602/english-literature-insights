The Age of Chaucer (1340–1400)

The Age of Chaucer represents the golden era of Middle English literature. This period is named after Geoffrey Chaucer, who is widely celebrated as the "Father of English Poetry". During this time, English firmly re-established itself as the language of high literature, blending Italian and French styles into a unique native voice.

Key Highlights of the Era:

  • The Rise of English: Shift from French or Latin to the East Midland dialect, which became the base of Modern English.
  • Social Realism: Literature moved away from pure fantasy/romance to portray real human experiences, irony, and social classes.
  • Historical Milestones: The era was shaped by major events like the Hundred Years' War, the Black Death (Plague), and the Peasants' Revolt.

Geoffrey Chaucer & Masterpieces

Chaucer’s crowning achievement is The Canterbury Tales, a brilliant collection of stories told by a diverse group of pilgrims traveling to Canterbury Cathedral. His other significant works include Troilus and Criseyde, The House of Fame, and The Parliament of Fowls.

Other Notable Contemporary Writers

While Chaucer dominated the era, other monumental writers also left their mark:

  • William Langland: Author of the famous allegorical poem Piers Plowman.
  • John Gower: Known for his work Confessio Amantis.
  • The Pearl Poet: The anonymous author of the timeless romance Sir Gawain and the Green Knight.
