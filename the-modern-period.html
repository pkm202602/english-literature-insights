1901 – 1939 A.D.

The Modern Period

An era of radical experimentation, stream of consciousness, WWI disillusionment, free verse, and profound psychological exploration.

Core Literary Movements & Innovations

Stream of Consciousness

Shifting from linear narrative to non-linear internal monologue, reflecting raw human thoughts and subconscious states.

Post-WWI Disillusionment & Fragmentation

Themes of alienation, spiritual breakdown, moral cynicism, and structural fragmentation following the devastation of World War I.

Imagism & Free Verse

Abandonment of traditional meter and rhyme schemes in favor of precise visual imagery, direct expression, and rhythmic liberty.

Rejection of Victorian Conventionality

Challenging Victorian moral hypocrisy, imperial propaganda, rigid class structures, and linear realism.

Sub-Periods & Literary Phases

1901 – 1910 A.D.

The Edwardian Period

Reign of King Edward VII. Transition era critiquing rigid class boundaries, imperialism, and social injustice in realistic prose.

Key Figures: George Bernard Shaw, H.G. Wells, E.M. Forster, and Joseph Conrad.
1910 – 1936 A.D.

The Georgian Period

Pre-war and interwar lyric poetry focusing on pastoral nature, rural life, formal simplicity, and nostalgic themes.

Key Figures: Rupert Brooke, Walter de la Mare, and W.H. Davies.
1914 – 1939 A.D.

High Modernism & War Poetry

Peak avant-garde experimentation driven by the psychological trauma of WWI, industrial technology, and urban complexity.

Key Figures: T.S. Eliot, Virginia Woolf, James Joyce, W.B. Yeats, and Wilfred Owen.
