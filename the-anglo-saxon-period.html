The Anglo-Saxon Period

The Anglo-Saxon Period focus deeply on the cultural, social, and historical impact of the Germanic tribes—specifically the Angles, Saxons, and Jutes—who settled in Britain starting from the 5th century. This era shaped the societal values that heavily influenced the literature of the Old English timeline.

Historical Background:

  • The Tribes: Arrival of the Angles, Saxons, and Jutes from Denmark and Germany.
  • The Heptarchy: The division of England into seven distinct kingdoms (Northumbria, Mercia, Wessex, etc.).
  • King Alfred the Great: A pivotal ruler of Wessex who saved England from Viking invasions and actively promoted the use of the Old English language in writing.

Culture and Society

Anglo-Saxon society was deeply tribal, organized around a king or chieftain and his loyal warriors (thanes). The community centered around the Mead-Hall, a place for feasting, drinking, and listening to the heroic tales of the scop. Their worldview was strongly influenced by the concept of Fate (Wyrd) and the pursuit of undying fame through brave deeds.
