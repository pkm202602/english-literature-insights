1660 – 1798 A.D.

The Neo-Classical Period

An era of Enlightenment rationalism, classical imitation, biting satire, social order, and the birth of the modern English novel.

Core Literary Movements & Innovations

Enlightenment & Rationalism

Prioritizing reason over emotion, scientific inquiry, logical order, and restraint over unchecked imagination.

Imitation of Classical Masters

Adherence to classical Roman and Greek aesthetic rules (Virgil, Horace) aiming for correctness, decorum, and balance.

Mock-Heroic Satire & Verse

Widespread use of the heroic couplet to criticize political corruption, moral hypocrisy, and social vanity.

Birth of Novels & Periodicals

Emergence of prose fiction, formal essay journalism (The Spectator), and comprehensive English lexicography.

Sub-Periods & Major Ages

1660 – 1700 A.D.

The Restoration Period

Monarchy restored under Charles II. Reopening of theatres introduces female actors and witty, cynical Comedy of Manners drama.

Key Figure: John Dryden (Age of Dryden), Aphra Behn, and William Congreve.
1700 – 1745 A.D.

The Augustan Period

Named after the prosperous Roman era of Emperor Augustus. Height of political satire, mock-epic poetry, and early realistic novels.

Key Figures: Alexander Pope (Age of Pope), Jonathan Swift, Daniel Defoe, and Joseph Addison.
1745 – 1798 A.D.

Age of Sensibility / Johnson

Transition period from strict Neo-Classical logic toward emotional expression, literary criticism, biography, and early Romantic pre-cursor poetry.

Key Figures: Dr. Samuel Johnson (Age of Johnson), James Boswell, Oliver Goldsmith, and Thomas Gray.
