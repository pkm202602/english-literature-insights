The Neo-Classical Period (1660–1785/1798)

The Neo-Classical Period is an era inspired by classical Greece and Rome. Driven by the **Age of Enlightenment**, writers of this time prioritized reason, order, logic, and restraint over the intense emotion and imagination of the previous Renaissance era. Literature became highly polished, satirical, and deeply analytical of human society.

To examine this expansive era, it is traditionally divided into three distinct sub-sections:

1. The Restoration Period (1660–1700)

Marked by the restoration of the English monarchy under King Charles II. This era saw the reopening of public theatres, the rise of the Comedy of Manners, and the heroic couplet. Dominated by figures like John Dryden.

2. The Augustan Period (1700–1745)

Also known as the Age of Pope, this sub-era drew heavy inspiration from the Roman Emperor Augustus. It was the golden age of political satire, wit, and early novels, championed by Alexander Pope and Jonathan Swift.

3. The Age of Sensibility (1745–1785/1798)

Commonly referred to as the Age of Johnson, this era served as a transition towards Romanticism. While emphasizing reason, it began to embrace instinct and feeling, featuring Samuel Johnson and the birth of Gothic fiction.
