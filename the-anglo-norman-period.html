The Anglo-Norman Period (1066–1340)

The Anglo-Norman Period began with the historic Norman Conquest in 1066, led by William the Conqueror. This victory introduced French culture and the Anglo-Norman language to the English court, splitting the literary landscape into three languages: French (for the aristocracy), Latin (for the church), and English (for the common folk).

Key Literary Themes & Developments:

  • Chivalric Romances: Tales of knights, honor, courtly love, and mythical quests (such as the legends of King Arthur).
  • Religious and Didactic Works: Literature aimed at teaching moral values and biblical stories to the common masses.
  • Chronicles: Historical writings in verse and prose that recorded the lineage and achievements of rulers.

Major Works and Influences

This period laid the foundation for the structural evolution of the English language. Famous literary examples include the *Brut* by Layamon (a historical poem about Britain's origins) and the widespread popularity of French *Fabliaux* (comic, satirical tales) and *Lais* (short romance poems).
