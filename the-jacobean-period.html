The Jacobean Period (1603–1625)

The Jacobean Period is named after King James I (Jacobus in Latin), who succeeded Queen Elizabeth I. This era marks a significant shift in English literature; the bright, optimistic romanticism of the Elizabethan times turned into a darker, more sophisticated, and deeply philosophical realism.

Key Characteristics:

  • Darker Themes: Literature and drama focused heavily on revenge, corruption, and the flaws of human nature.
  • Metaphysical Poetry: The rise of highly intellectualized poetry using far-fetched metaphors (conceits).
  • The King James Bible (1611): A monumental prose achievement that standardised English prose style.

Sub-section: Age of Shakespeare (1590–1616)

While William Shakespeare started his career in the Elizabethan era, his most profound, complex, and mature works were written and performed during the Jacobean era. This peak period defined English tragic theatre.

  • The Great Tragedies: Masterpieces like Hamlet, Macbeth, Othello, and King Lear were produced, capturing intense psychological depth.
  • Tragicomedies & Romances: Late plays like The Tempest and The Winter's Tale blended dark tones with reconciliation.

Sub-section: Puritan Period (1620–1660)

Overlapping with the later sub-eras of the Renaissance, the **Puritan Period** reflects the powerful religious, social, and political influence of the Puritans in England, advocating for moral purity and structural reform in church and state.

  • John Milton: The greatest literary voice of this movement, laying the foundations for his epic thoughts.
  • Closure of Theatres (1642): Public stages were banned due to political conflicts and Puritan moral views, leading to a massive decline in English drama.

Other Major Figures of the Era

This period also features John Donne (the pioneer of Metaphysical Poetry) and Ben Jonson (famous for his satirical comedies and comedies of humours like Volpone).
