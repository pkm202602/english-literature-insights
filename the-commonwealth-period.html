The Commonwealth Period (1649–1660)

The Commonwealth Period, also known as the Puritan Interregnum, represents a unique decade in English history when England was without a monarch. Following the execution of King Charles I, the nation was governed as a republic under the leadership of Oliver Cromwell. This political and religious shift heavily impacted the literary world.

Key Characteristics & Literary Environment:

  • The Theatre Ban: Public theatres remained strictly closed, which completely dissolved the era of professional playwriting and drama.
  • Puritan Dominance: Literature shifted towards strict religious, political, and moral discourses instead of courtly romance.
  • Rise of Prose: Pamphlets, political essays, and religious treatises became the dominant forms of expression.

Towering Literary Figures

1. John Milton

The undisputed literary giant of this era. Milton served as the Latin Secretary to Cromwell's Council of State. During this period, he wrote powerful political tracts and prose works like Areopagitica (a passionate defense of freedom of speech). This era also laid the psychological and philosophical foundation for his later masterpiece, Paradise Lost.

2. Thomas Hobbes

A major political philosopher who published his monumental work Leviathan in 1551, which discussed the structure of society and legitimate government, deeply reflecting the chaos of the civil war era.

3. Andrew Marvell

A transitional poet who successfully balanced metaphysical complexity with political loyalty. He wrote famous poems dedicated to Cromwell, such as An Horatian Ode upon Cromwell's Return from Ireland.
