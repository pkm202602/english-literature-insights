1500 – 1660 A.D.

The Renaissance & Early Modern Period

A glorious rebirth of classical learning, humanism, artistic innovation, and the golden age of English drama and poetry.

Core Literary Movements & Innovations

Humanism & Secularism

A shift from medieval scholasticism to focusing on human potential, individualism, and classical Greek and Latin literature.

Golden Age of Drama

The rise of public theatres (The Globe, The Rose), blank verse tragedy, comedy of humours, and revenge plays.

Poetic Forms & Sonnets

Introduction and perfection of the English (Shakespearean) and Petrarchan sonnets, epic allegories, and pastoral poetry.

Prose & Philosophical Inquiry

Empirical philosophy, personal essays, translation of the King James Bible (1611), and utopian political thought.

Sub-Periods & Major Ages

1500 – 1558 A.D.

Preparation of Renaissance

The early groundwork introducing printing, Italian literary influences, and humanism to England under Henry VII and Henry VIII.

Pioneers: Sir Thomas More (Utopia), Thomas Wyatt, and Earl of Surrey.
1558 – 1603 A.D.

The Elizabethan Period

The pinnacle of English literary renaissance during Queen Elizabeth I's reign. Marked by national pride, poetry, and tragic drama.

Key Figures: Edmund Spenser, Christopher Marlowe, Sir Philip Sidney, and Early Shakespeare.
1603 – 1625 A.D.

The Jacobean Period

Coinciding with King James I. Characterized by darker tragedies, revenge drama, metaphysical poetry, and the King James Bible.

Sub-Age: Age of Shakespeare (1590–1616 A.D.) — The height of Shakespearean tragedy & comedy.
1625 – 1649 A.D.

The Caroline Period

Reign of King Charles I. Marked by rising political and religious tensions between Royalist Cavaliers and Puritan Parliamentarians.

Key Figures: Cavalier Poets (Robert Herrick, Richard Lovelace) and John Ford.
1649 – 1660 A.D.

The Commonwealth Period

Also known as the Puritan Interregnum under Oliver Cromwell. Public theatres were closed (1642–1660), shifting focus to political prose.

Key Figures: John Milton (political pamphlets), Thomas Hobbes (Leviathan), and Andrew Marvell.
1620 – 1660 A.D.

The Puritan Period

An overlapping religious and moral movement spanning the late Jacobean, Caroline, and Commonwealth eras, demanding moral reform and religious devotion.
