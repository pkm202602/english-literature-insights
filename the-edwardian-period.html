The Edwardian Period (1901 – 1914)

Overview

The Edwardian period in the United Kingdom marks the reign of King Edward VII following the death of Queen Victoria. It spans from the turn of the 20th century until the outbreak of World War I. This era is often characterized by its unique social changes, the rise of the women's suffrage movement, and significant advancements in technology and science.

Literature and Culture

Edwardian literature often reflected a growing divide between the rigid social hierarchies of the Victorian era and the fast-paced, industrializing modern world. Notable writers of this era include George Bernard Shaw, H.G. Wells, and E.M. Forster.
