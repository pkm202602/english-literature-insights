1. Old (Ancient) English Period

450 – 1066 A.D.

The dawn of English literature following the Germanic migrations to Britain. Characterized by heroic oral poetry, scop recitations, Christian monastic scribes, and manuscripts such as Beowulf.

The Anglo-Saxon Period 450 – 1066 A.D. Tribal culture, King Alfred & Wyrd

2. Middle English Period

1066 – 1500 A.D.

Transitional period sparked by the Norman Conquest of 1066. Witnessed the transformation of Old English into Middle English, chivalric romances, social realism, and early printing.

The Anglo-Norman Period 1066 – 1340 A.D. French influence & Arthurian romance The Age of Chaucer 1340 – 1400 A.D. The Canterbury Tales & social satire Dark / Barren Period 1400 – 1485/1500 A.D. Caxton printing press & Malory's prose

3. The Renaissance / Early Modern Period

1500 – 1660 A.D.

The "rebirth" of classical learning, humanism, and artistic expression. Brought an unprecedented boom in Elizabethan drama, sonnets, metaphysical poetry, and monumental prose.

Preparation for Renaissance 1500 – 1558 A.D. Early Tudors, Sir Thomas More & Sonnets The Elizabethan Period 1558 – 1603 A.D. Golden Age of Drama & Marlowe The Jacobean Period 1603 – 1625 A.D. King James Bible & Metaphysical poetry Age of Shakespeare 1590 – 1616 A.D. The Four Great Tragedies & Sonnets The Caroline Period 1625 – 1649 A.D. Cavalier Poets & Carpe Diem verse The Commonwealth Period 1649 – 1660 A.D. Puritan Interregnum & Milton's prose Puritan Period (Overlapping) 1620 – 1660 A.D. John Milton & Pilgrim's Progress

4. Neo-Classical Period

1660 – 1798 A.D.

An age of reason, order, classicism, and sharp social satire modelled after classical Roman masters. Marked by the emergence of English journalism, coffee-house culture, and early novel forms.

The Restoration Period 1660 – 1700 A.D. Age of Dryden & Comedy of Manners The Augustan Period 1700 – 1745 A.D. Age of Pope, Swift & early novel The Age of Sensibility 1745 – 1785/1798 A.D. Age of Dr. Johnson & Gothic horror

5. Romantic Period

1798 – 1832 A.D.

The Golden Age of English Poetry. A passionate rebellion against Enlightenment rationalism and industrialization, prioritizing raw emotion, nature, imagination, and individual liberty.

Romantic Masters & Poets 1798 – 1832 A.D. Wordsworth, Coleridge, Keats, Shelley & Byron

6. Victorian Period

1832 – 1901 A.D.

Defined by the reign of Queen Victoria, industrial expansion, science vs. religion conflicts, and the golden rise of the English social novel (Charles Dickens, Brontë sisters).

The Pre-Raphaelites 1848 – 1860 A.D. Sensuous art, symbolism & Rossettis Aestheticism and Decadence 1880 – 1901 A.D. "Art for Art's Sake" & Oscar Wilde

7. Modern Period (1st Half of 20th Century)

1901 – 1939 A.D.

A radical aesthetic revolution triggered by World War I and technological transformation. Writers embraced "Make it new!", stream of consciousness, broken chronologies, and post-war disillusionment.

The Edwardian Period 1901 – 1910 A.D. Social criticism & class divides The Georgian Period 1910 – 1936 A.D. Pastoral poetry before WWI

8. Post-Modern & Contemporary Period

1939 – Present

Break from grand narratives into metafiction, irony, absurdism, post-colonial voices, climate fiction (cli-fi), digital hyper-reality, and diverse global perspectives.

The Post-Modern Period 1939 – Present Beckett, Orwell, Vonnegut & Metafiction The Contemporary Period 2000 – Present Cli-Fi, Digital narratives & Global identity
