1939 – 2000 A.D.

The Post-Modern Period

An era marked by metafiction, fragmented narratives, existential absurdism, post-colonial perspectives, pastiche, and the questioning of objective truth.

Core Literary Features & Innovations

Metafiction & Self-Reflexivity

Works that draw attention to their own status as crafted artifacts, playing with the relationship between fiction and reality.

Pastiche, Irony & Black Comedy

Combining elements from multiple genres and styles while examining modern anxieties with satirical, dark humor.

Magical Realism & Mythmaking

Blending realistic storytelling with fantastical, supernatural elements presented as everyday occurrences.

Post-Colonial & Global Voices

Dismantling Eurocentric narratives, addressing decolonization, identity, displacement, and cultural hybridization.

Major Literary Movements & Phases

1940s – 1960s A.D.

Theatre of the Absurd

Drama reflecting the absurdity of human existence in a post-WWII world, featuring repetitive dialogue and existential crisis.

Key Figures: Samuel Beckett, Eugène Ionesco, and Harold Pinter.
1950s – 1970s A.D.

Beat Generation & Angry Young Men

Rebellion against post-war materialism, rigid social class standards, and conventional literary forms.

Key Figures: Jack Kerouac, Allen Ginsberg, John Osborne, and Kingsley Amis.
1960s – 2000 A.D.

Metafiction & Post-Colonial Literature

Complex narrative structures questioning linear history, imperial legacies, and master narratives on a global scale.

Key Figures: Gabriel García Márquez, Salman Rushdie, Toni Morrison, and Thomas Pynchon.
