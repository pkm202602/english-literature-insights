The Pre-Raphaelites

The Pre-Raphaelite Brotherhood (PRB) was a unique and revolutionary group of English painters, poets, and critics founded in 1848. They strongly rejected the mechanistic, artificial approach to art and poetry that was promoted by the Royal Academy, which heavily followed the styles of the Renaissance master Raphael. Instead, they wanted to bring back the **detailed, vibrant, and deeply symbolic art style** that existed before Raphael.

Key Characteristics & Philosophy:

  • Sensuous Detail: Poetry and art filled with precise, glowing visual details and intense color imagery.
  • Medieval Mysticism: A deep attraction to Arthurian legends, religious devotion, and medieval romance themes.
  • Pictorial Poetry: Writing poems that painted vivid, photographic pictures in the reader's mind, creating a strong bond between painting and verse.

Towering Giants of the Brotherhood

1. Dante Gabriel Rossetti (1828–1882)

The central figure who was equally brilliant as a painter and a poet. Rossetti's poetry is famous for its rich sensuousness, spiritual symbolism, and emotional depth. His immortal poem "The Blessed Damozel" beautifully showcases his hallmark style of mixing heavenly spirit with earthly love.

2. Christina Rossetti (1830–1894)

Dante Gabriel’s sister and one of the greatest female poets of the Victorian era. She wrote with beautiful simplicity and deep emotion. Her masterpiece narrative poem "Goblin Market" is widely celebrated for its rich, mysterious symbolism, fairy-tale structure, and sub-textual layers.

3. William Morris

A multi-talented artist, designer, and poet who fought against industrial ugliness. He revived medieval crafts and wrote epic narrative poetry like The Earthly Paradise, contributing immensely to the aesthetic value of the age.
