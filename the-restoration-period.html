The Restoration Period (1660–1700)

The Restoration Period began with the restoration of the English monarchy and the return of King Charles II from his exile in France. This era brought a massive cultural shift as the strict moral codes of the Puritan regime were replaced by French wit, fashion, and sophisticated loose lifestyles.

Key Literary Developments:

  • Reopening of Theatres: Charles II instantly legalized public drama, introducing women to the English stage for the first time.
  • Comedy of Manners: A sharp, witty genre of drama that satirized the artificial manners and sexual intrigues of upper-class society.
  • The Heroic Couplet: A traditional verse form of rhymed iambic pentameter that became the primary vehicle for poetry and satire.

Towering Figures of the Era

1. John Dryden (1631–1700)

The undisputed champion of the era, so much so that it is often called the Age of Dryden. He was England's first official Poet Laureate. Masterworks like his satirical poem Absalom and Achitophel and the play All for Love defined the literary standards of his time.

2. William Congreve & Aphra Behn

Congreve wrote the brilliant comedy of manners The Way of the World. Meanwhile, Aphra Behn broke historical barriers as one of the first professional English women writers, famous for her novel Oroonoko.
