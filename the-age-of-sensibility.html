The Age of Sensibility (1745–1785/1798)

The Age of Sensibility, widely referred to as the Age of Johnson, marks the final phase of the Neo-Classical era. This sub-period served as a critical cultural bridge; while it maintained the Neo-Classical respect for reason and order, it increasingly emphasized human instinct, emotion, nature, and raw feeling (sensibility), directly paving the path toward the Romantic movement.

Key Milestones & Transitional Features:

  • The Rise of Literary Criticism: Literature became highly academic, structured, and critically analyzed through dictionaries and essays.
  • The Growth of the Novel: The novel format matured rapidly, exploring complex domestic and human psychology.
  • Birth of Gothic Fiction: Introduction of stories focusing on terror, mystery, dark castles, and supernatural elements.

Towering Giants of the Transition

1. Samuel Johnson (1709–1784)

The monumental literary figure who dominated this age. Dr. Johnson single-handedly compiled the definitive A Dictionary of the English Language (1755), which revolutionized and standardized English spelling, meanings, and syntax rules forever.

2. The Novelists & Early Gothic Masters

This age featured great novelists like Henry Fielding (author of Tom Jones). It also birthed Gothic fiction through Horace Walpole, who wrote the legendary first gothic horror novel, The Castle of Otranto (1764).

3. Precursors to Romanticism

Poets like Thomas Gray (famous for his solemn poem Elegy Written in a Country Churchyard) broke away from urban wit to write about rural life, grief, and nature, laying the foundational seeds for Romanticism.
